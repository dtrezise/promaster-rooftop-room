The product vision

A second room—
without a second vehicle.

The concept treats the van roof as a living-space platform, not simply a place to attach a conventional sleeping tent. The product is still at feasibility stage.

01

Use the whole roof

A full-length cassette turns the available roof footprint into a large, continuous room rather than a compact two-person sleeping shell.

02

Stand naturally

The target is genuine standing height throughout the central room—not the 39–48 inch interior common to rooftop tents.

03

Close the weather out

Insulated panels, thermally managed joints, compression seals and controlled ventilation define a measurable four-season performance objective.

04

Let the structure deploy itself

Air-assisted motion and synchronized actuators perform the work; rigid posts, beams and mechanical locks carry the final loads.

Candidate mechanism architecture

Pneumatic origami.
Mechanical certainty.

Inflation may assist motion and seal preload, but it cannot be the occupied or travel load path. Hard panels must meet rigid frames and mechanically lock before occupancy.

  1. 1

    Lift

    Four synchronized telescoping posts raise the rigid roof shell to standing height.

  2. 2

    Unfold

    Segmented insulated wall leaves rotate into place with pneumatic hinge assistance.

  3. 3

    Lock

    Over-center latches and structural mullions establish the occupied wind and snow load paths.

  4. 4

    Seal

    Inflatable perimeter gaskets preload every panel joint against rain, drafts and thermal leakage.

The governing rule

Air moves it.
Mechanics hold it.

The credible first product

One vision.
Two interface paths.

A roughly 13 × 6 foot room provides about 78 square feet, but the published ProMaster roof guidance limits the roof carrier and load to 150 kg. The program must now prove either a mass-compliant accessory or a qualified integrated upfit.

  • Freeze vehicle and product requirements before detailed design
  • Build a bottom-up mass model with weighed coupons
  • Obtain written roof-interface limits from Ram and an upfitter
  • Prototype one structural bay before a vehicle-scale system
  • Keep internal access outside the first proof vehicle
Path A · strict mass gate

Roof-carrier accessory

≤330lb including rack

Preserves the no-drill accessory model, but the complete travel system must remain within the published roof-load envelope and per-attachment limits.

Path B · qualified alteration

Integrated elevating-roof upfit

650–950lb screening range

May preserve the hard-wall experience, but requires an engineered body load path, qualified installation, compliance review and a materially higher installed price.

Feasibility verdict

Product concept plausible.
Vehicle fit unresolved.

The experience is coherent and the mechanism is plausible. The current hard-wall mass screening is incompatible with the published roof-carrier envelope, so the interface architecture is the first release gate.

The defining constraint

Hard walls change the mass equation.

A roof-footprint room contains roughly 344 square feet of walls and roof before counting the floor. At a screening assumption of 1.5 lb/ft², the enclosure skin alone is about 516 pounds. A complete hard-wall system currently screens near 850–950 pounds, far above the 330-pound published roof carrier and load limit. These figures identify the decision gate; they are not released design values.

Development roadmap

Resolve the interface.
Then prove one bay.

The fastest credible path begins with vehicle data, mass and architecture. It then advances through increasingly representative physical tests.

  1. A

    Gate 0 · 30 days

    Requirements and vehicle evidence

    Record actual axle weights, scan the roof, map every attachment, obtain written interface guidance and freeze the v2 requirement set.

  2. B

    Gate 1 · 60 days

    Architecture and mass decision

    Compare a sub-330-pound accessory with an integrated upfit using packaging CAD, weighed coupons, load paths and supplier evidence.

  3. C

    Gate 2 · prototype

    Quarter-scale and one-bay rigs

    Resolve kinematics first, then cycle a full-size post, wall leaves, locks, seals, controls and manual recovery system.

  4. D

    Gates 3–5 · staged release

    Vehicle proof, environment proof, pilot

    Advance only after measured release criteria for travel restraint, vehicle reactions, weather, thermal performance, life cycle and human safety.

The opportunity

Not a rooftop tent.
A new room for the van.

The concept is ambitious because it combines the space of an integrated pop-top with a low-profile travel form and assisted deployment. The business opportunity depends on one result: a tested vehicle architecture that carries the experience without exceeding its load, safety and service limits.
